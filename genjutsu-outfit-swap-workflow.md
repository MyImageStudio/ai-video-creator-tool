# Seedance 2.5: a tested video-and-image guidance workflow

An adult courier, a red delivery bag and a cake worth protecting. The source has a burgundy jacket; the guided take has a cobalt-blue jacket. This case includes the actual inputs, successful prompt, complete result and a short comparison edit.

**Scope:** this is an internal AI model demonstration, with fictional adults and staged, non-graphic action. It is one successful take, not a benchmark or a customer delivery case. Guidance changed movement, timing and small details as well as the jacket.

## 2. Describe the new scene, then assign each input its job

The test requested **Seedance 2.5, 15 seconds, 720p, portrait 9:16**, with the source video as action/camera guidance and the still image as appearance guidance. These are the test settings, not a promise that every provider offers the same input mode.

An earlier request phrased as an edit failed without returning a video. This guided-scene prompt produced the linked take:

```text
A 15-second vertical 9:16 photorealistic action-comedy scene in a daylight concrete parking garage. An adult female courier wears a cobalt-blue leather jacket, black shirt and dark trousers, carrying a bright red insulated delivery bag. A male attacker lunges; she ducks the punch and redirects him onto a luggage trolley beside a parked car. She opens the red bag to reveal an intact white cake, then tastes it with a fork. Use the supplied video as action and camera guidance, and the supplied image as the courier appearance reference. Clear adult faces, realistic body weight and readable physical movement, natural daylight, cinematic handheld framing, normal speed, a complete comic payoff. No blood, weapons, text or logos.
```

For a new attempt, keep the roles explicit: **video → action and camera guidance; image → appearance guidance**. Describe the action, setting, lighting and payoff in ordinary scene language. Treat the inputs as guidance rather than a guarantee of an identical performance.

## 3. Review the result before cutting a highlight

- Watch both complete clips. Check the courier's jacket, bag, body contact and the cake reveal.
- Compare faces, gestures, camera movement and timing. Do not infer identity or motion preservation from one matching frame.
- Check small props. The cake's appearance changes; the source cherry is missing in the guided take.
- Listen to the complete audio and inspect synchronization. An audio stream alone does not prove usable sound.
- Check the encoded dimensions. The delivered files are 720 × 1280; native internal generation resolution was not independently verified. An “8K” prompt tag would not establish an 8K file.

Keep normal speed and original audio, use full-frame fit, and export at **720 × 1280**. The example uses no transitions, added music or local upscaling. Its nominal timeline is 13.2 seconds. The first edit cut to the cake too early; moving the reveal to 10.2–11.3s made the payoff readable.

These cuts can be entered in an editor that supports trimming and captions. Our linked example was assembled in SeedanceCheap Studio 2's native Video Editor. Generation, reference creation and editing are separate activities.
